### F-1: A row created by a Settled grill decision is malformed by construction — the two-entry `Propagation sites` floor cannot be met for the findings a grill actually defers
**Severity:** P1
**Where:** spec § Design 1 (spec.md:96), § D15 (spec.md:65, :100), row shape (spec.md:118), § Design 7 block (spec.md:182)
**Edge case:** The operator takes 2f option 4 at the round-4 halt, and a Settled decision dispositions a remaining finding as a follow-up. `skills/spec-cycle/SKILL.md:544` applies Settled items "under the 2e rounds-1–3 rules", and spec.md:96 says routing's tests **do not run** there — "a Settled grill decision is applied as written … satisfied by appending a row in the shape below with `Deferred in: round 4 (grill)`".
**What happens:** The shape the row must take (spec.md:118) requires `Propagation sites:` to list "two or more entries; … at least one must exist", and the reviewers' well-formedness test enforces exactly that (spec.md:182). But the grill deferral that most needs this path is a *one-site* finding: a 2+-site deferrable finding would already have been routed to a row by round 4's own routing step, so what survives to the grill and gets deferred by operator decision is disproportionately the single-site class the ceiling would otherwise force to fold. Because the routing tests are switched off, nothing produces a second site, and nothing tells the author what to write in `Scope:` either (step 2 never ran).

The row is written, 2f-i step 5 re-renders the halt block, the run ends. On the operator's re-run (2f menu option 1, the documented path back into the loop), **round 1** — the block is ungated — every dispatched lens finds a row with one propagation site and files `routing violation: D-<n>` P0. Costs, in order of severity:
- The only repairs R2 permits are (a) "repairing the field a `routing violation: D-<n>` finding names" — here that means **inventing a second propagation site** so the row passes, i.e. falsifying the record the next reviewer reads — or dropping the row by hand outside the loop (spec.md:77), which the loop never solicits and which discards the operator's own decision. R2(c)'s discharge is not available: it is conditioned on `P0` + `in-scope`, so a P1 grill row has no discharge path at all.
- If the operator's Settled finding *was* an in-scope P0 (common at round 4 — P0s are what survive), the row is a ceiling violation whatever its site count, and the only in-loop remedy, R2(c), is to **fold the finding at every site** — silently reversing the operator's Settled decision, in a skill whose brief says the operator's decision is the disposition.
- Either way the P0 is filed by up to four lenses on round 1 of the re-run, so the re-run starts at `total_p0p1 ≥ 1` on a defect that is an artifact of the mechanism.
**Why the spec misses it:** Line 96 was written to answer "does routing gate the grill?" (it does not) and stops there. The symmetrical question — the reviewers' tests are *not* switched off for grill rows, and they are stricter than what the grill path can produce — was never asked. D15 authorizes the append at round 4 (spec.md:100) and § Minor additions repeats the exemption (spec.md:77), but no clause reconciles a routing-exempt row with a routing-derived well-formedness contract. Note also that `skills/spec-cycle/SKILL.md:545` already uses "discharged" in the grill sense the spec deliberately reworded away from, so the two senses will still co-exist in the shipped file.
**Suggested fix:** Make the grill row a declared variant in both the producer and the consumer, one sentence each. In the 2e insertion at spec.md:96: "A row whose `Deferred in:` carries `(grill)` records the operator's disposition, not a routing outcome: `Propagation sites:` lists whatever sites the finding names — one entry is permitted, and `§ (operator decision — routing tests not run)` when the finding names none — and `Scope:` is written `operator — round-4 grill` when step 2 did not run." In the § Design 7 block (spec.md:182), after the well-formedness sentence: "A row whose `Deferred in:` field carries `(grill)` is exempt from the two-entry `Propagation sites` floor and from the ceiling test; it records an operator decision and is checked only for the presence of its fields." Add to checklist row 6 `grep -cF '(grill)'` ≥ 1 per agent and to row 8 the same for `SKILL.md`.

### F-2: Step 4's discharge fires on any cited row and folds only the new finding's sites — R2(c) permits neither, and a live P1/out-of-scope row is silently marked resolved
**Severity:** P1
**Where:** spec § Design 1 step 4 (spec.md:94) against § Design 2 R2(c) (spec.md:127), § Design 7 block (spec.md:182), § Design 5 (spec.md:167)
**Edge case:** A reviewer takes the D14 exception. The block's rule is unconditional on the row's own fields: "Do not file a P0/P1 whose root is a well-formed row … **unless the candidate is an in-scope P0**, which is always filed **with the row cited as context**" (spec.md:182). So a row recorded `P1` / `out-of-scope` gets cited whenever some lens's *candidate* on that root is an in-scope P0 — which is the normal case for a broad root (the row captured the wide, out-of-scope version; the candidate is the narrow, in-scope, unshippable slice of it).
**What happens:** Two divergences between the trigger and the rule it invokes:

1. **Precondition.** Step 4: "If the finding cites a row `D-<n>` … the fold **is** that row's discharge under R2(c): append `**Discharged:** …` to the row in the same round." No condition on the row. R2(c) (spec.md:127) discharges only "a row whose `Finding` severity is `P0` and whose `Scope` is, or becomes under an (a) repair, `in-scope`." For a cited P1 or out-of-scope row the two shipped sentences give the 2e author contradictory instructions — step 4 mandates the append, R2(c) does not authorize it. (Read strictly, that is an internal inconsistency between two rules in the same insertion; I file it P1 rather than P0 because R2(c) can be read as the governing precondition and step 4 as the trigger.)
2. **Site list.** Step 4 folds "every listed site" — the *new finding's* step-1 list — and writes those sections into `Discharged:`. R2(c) requires folding "at every site on **its** `Propagation sites` list", i.e. the row's. The lens verifies only what the field names ("A `Discharged:` field is verified like a `fixed:` disposition: every section it names carries the fold"), so a fold covering a strict subset of the row's sites passes verification.

Combined, the failure is silent and terminal for the deferred finding: the row now carries `Discharged:`, so (a) the render prints ` [discharged: …]` and the spec says it "needs no ticket" (spec.md:167) — the operator drops it from the follow-up list; (b) it is still well-formed, so D14 suppression keeps every lens from re-filing the residual defect; (c) it is no longer a same-root target for the author (spec.md:94), so a later defer appends a `(recurrence of D-<m>)` row instead of repairing it; and (d) R2 forbids rewriting the finding record, and nothing permits removing `Discharged:`. A P1 or out-of-scope P0 the spec deliberately recorded as a known issue is therefore marked resolved by a fold that never addressed it — precisely the "silently dropped finding" § Design 6 (spec.md:174) names as the failure mode this machinery exists to prevent.
**Why the spec misses it:** The round-2 fix wired step 4's fold branch to R2(c) from the *fold* side (the author has just folded; the row must be discharged), and R2(c) was broadened from the *row* side (state-based trigger) in the same round. Each was checked against the in-scope-P0-row case they were both written for; neither was walked against a citation of a row that is not an in-scope P0 row, which the block's exception produces by design.
**Suggested fix:** Two clauses. In step 4 (spec.md:94): "…the fold is also that row's discharge **when the row itself records a P0 with `Scope: in-scope`** — R2(c)'s precondition; fold at the union of this finding's sites and the row's `Propagation sites`, and name every one of them in `**Discharged:**`. If the cited row records anything else, it is not discharged: fold the finding at its own sites, disposition it `fixed: edited § <a>; § <b>`, and leave the row live." Mirror one sentence in the § Design 7 block so the lens can see the distinction: "A row cited by an in-scope-P0 filing must carry `Discharged:` by the next round **only when the row itself records a P0 with `Scope: in-scope`**; a cited P1 or out-of-scope row stays live and is not expected to carry the field."

### F-3: The fence rule fixes what opens a fence but not what *closes* one — a nested or documented fence still flips parity and hides the whole section
**Severity:** P2
**Pre-ship recommended:** yes
**Where:** spec § Design 2 (spec.md:104), § Design 7 block (spec.md:182), inherited by § Design 5 (spec.md:167)
**Edge case:** The round-2 fix reads: "A fence marker counts only when three or more backticks or tildes begin the line (after at most three spaces) and the line is not prefixed `> `, **matched from the top of the file**." That is a parity toggle over markers. It never says a closing fence must use the **same character** as its opener, or be **at least as long**, or that a marker *inside* an open fence cannot open another. CommonMark says all three; the spec's grammar, which the reader is told to apply instead, does not.
**What happens:** The specimen is a spec that documents markdown — this repo's entire subject matter, and both the specimens the brief cites (VHS-33, VHS-41) are prose-only markdown specs. A four-backtick block showing a three-backtick example is the standard way to write that:

````text
`````markdown
```markdown
## Deferred — follow-up required
```
`````
````
Under the spec's rule the outer opener toggles *on*, the inner marker toggles *off*, and the outer closer toggles *on* again — parity ends **open**, so every line after it, including `## Deferred — follow-up required` and every `### D-<n>:` row, is "inside a fenced code block": never a heading, never a row, never a terminator. A tilde block containing a backtick line does the same. Consequences are identical to the ones round-2 F-2 was filed for and are all silent: Design 5 renders `(none)`; every reviewer's block finds no section, so D14 suppression stops firing and **every deferred finding is re-filed** — the oscillation the ticket exists to stop; a mid-section flip leaves the surviving rows checked and the rest unchecked. The one mitigation added this round is diagnostic only: § Design 6 (spec.md:174) now tells the operator to check fence parity when `(none)` appears unexpectedly.

Filed P2 rather than P1 — the deliberate arbitrary-text path (`Suggested fix`) is now safe, so nothing in the mechanism *manufactures* the trigger; it needs the spec's own prose to nest fences. But it is the same silent, total failure, and the fix is one clause.
**Why the spec misses it:** Round-2 F-2 asked for the two missing predicates on the *opening* side (line-start, not blockquoted) and got exactly those two. Fence *pairing* was never in the sentence to begin with, so nothing prompted a re-read of it.
**Suggested fix:** Extend the byte-identical clause in both places (spec.md:104, :182): "…matched from the top of the file: a marker opens a fence only when no fence is open, and closes one only when it uses the same character as the opener and is at least as long; a shorter or different marker inside an open fence is content." Add to checklist rows 6 and 8: `grep -cF 'same character as the opener'` ≥ 1 per agent and in `SKILL.md`.

### F-4: `routing violation:` findings are exempt from routing but not from the recount, and every violation after the first in an invocation lands on a section a prior disposition already named
**Severity:** P2
**Pre-ship recommended:** yes
**Where:** spec § Design 3 (spec.md:135-139) against § Design 1 step 0 (spec.md:86)
**Edge case:** Two or more `routing violation:` findings in one invocation — routine, since every lens files them independently and rows accumulate across rounds.
**What happens:** Step 0 removes a routing violation from routing ("never deferred … whatever its site count or scope") and prescribes dispositions that all carry the anchor `(§ Deferred — follow-up required)`; the ceiling-class one also names the fold sections. Design 3 then runs *before* routing on every new P0/P1 and matches its `Where` against "every section named on any `fixed:` / `reworked:` line of the manifests built earlier in this invocation". A routing violation's `Where` is by construction the deferral section, and every earlier violation's disposition names that section — so the recount fires on essentially every violation after the first, against a finding step 0 explicitly took out of the routing machinery. Two bad outcomes:
- **Common case:** both findings resolve to the same one section, count stays at 1, so the "Otherwise" bullet applies and the author "patch[es] once more with the corrected site list" on a *row repair* that needs no re-patching, tagging the disposition `(recount: 1 sites, fold kept)`. Noise the next reviewer must adjudicate, and a re-edit of a field that was already correct.
- **Worse case:** if the author counts two different rows as two sites (the spec's site vocabulary is `§ <section>`, but "every place in the spec the fix must land" invites per-row counting), the count reaches two and the revert branch is reachable. Its guard excludes only "an in-scope P0"; a routing violation's scope is never classified, because step 0 skips step 2. The branch then **reverts the earlier row repair** and defers the routing violation as a row — a row whose root is "row D-3 is malformed". From the next round on, D14 suppresses re-filing of that root, so the malformed row D-3 is permanently masked by row D-6, and the spec ships a follow-up section that hides its own defect. Note that a **discharge** disposition drags its fold sections into the same trap: `fixed: discharged D-<n> — edited § <a>; § <b>` names real spec sections, so a later finding on `§ <a>` recounts against a finding that is by definition an in-scope P0 — correctly blocked from revert, but still routed through a re-patch of an already-discharged finding.
**Why the spec misses it:** Step 0's exemption and Design 3's recount were written in different rounds against different problems, and Design 3's trigger is textual (does the `Where` land on a named section?) while step 0's exemption is title-based. Nothing links them, and the fact that *all* routing-violation dispositions anchor the same section makes the collision systematic rather than incidental.
**Suggested fix:** One sentence at the head of Design 3, mirroring step 0: "The recount does not run on a finding whose title begins `routing violation:`, and a disposition whose only named section is `§ Deferred — follow-up required` is not a recount trigger — a row repair is not a fold." If the discharge case should keep triggering the recount on its fold sections, say so explicitly in the same sentence, and state that a discharged finding resolves by the 'Otherwise' bullet (the existing in-scope-P0 guard already forbids reverting it).

### F-5: A row's recorded severity can never be raised, so an out-of-scope P0 suppressed by a P1 row is under-prioritized in the only surface the operator sees
**Severity:** P3
**Where:** spec § Design 2 R2 (spec.md:127), § Design 7 block (spec.md:182), § Design 5 (spec.md:157)
**Edge case:** A root is deferred at P1 in round 1. In round 3 a lens's candidate on the same root is a **P0** that is *out of scope* — so the D14 exception (in-scope P0 only) does not apply and the finding is suppressed by the well-formed row.
**What happens:** The right routing outcome is reached (an out-of-scope P0 defers at any severity under the ceiling), but the row still reads `(P1)`, and R2 closes the door on correcting it: "The finding record — `Finding`, `Deferred in`, `Suggested fix` — is never rewritten." The follow-up report prints severity from that field (spec.md:157), so the operator files and triages a P0-severity follow-up as a P1. Degrades gracefully — nothing breaks, the row is still there — but the report's severity column is the one signal the operator uses to order the follow-ups.
**Why the spec misses it:** The immutability of the finding record was written to protect provenance against re-anchoring edits; severity was bundled into it without asking whether a later round can learn that the recorded severity is too low.
**Suggested fix:** Carve severity out of the freeze, as an appended note rather than a rewrite: in R2, "A later round may append ` (raised to P0 round <n> by <lens>/R<n>/F-<k>)` to the `Finding:` field's parenthetical when a suppressed same-root candidate carried a higher severity; nothing else in the finding record is ever rewritten." Design 5 then prints the raised severity; the block's ceiling test reads the raised value, which is the conservative direction.

### F-6: `Discharged:` names spec sections but has no re-anchoring path, so a later rename turns a settled row into an unclassified verification failure
**Severity:** P3
**Where:** spec § Design 2 R2(b)/(c) (spec.md:127), § Design 7 block (spec.md:182)
**Edge case:** A row is discharged in round 2; in round 3 a fold renames or merges one of the sections `Discharged:` names — the same event R2(b) exists to absorb for `Where` and `Propagation sites`.
**What happens:** The block says "A `Discharged:` field is verified like a `fixed:` disposition: every section it names carries the fold." The named section no longer exists, so the check fails — and the spec never says what a lens files when it does. It is not part of the well-formedness enumeration (the eighth field is optional and unconstrained there), and the ceiling sentence covers only rows *without* the field. Meanwhile R2(b)'s re-anchoring is scoped to "an entry in `Where` or `Propagation sites`" — `Discharged:` is not listed, so on a literal reading the author may not fix it either; the only route is to read R2(a) ("repairing the field a `routing violation: D-<n>` finding names") as covering a field the reviewer had no defined finding class for. Cost is a round of adjudication, not a deadlock, which is why this is P3.
**Why the spec misses it:** `Discharged:` was added as a terminal marker — written once, never touched again — and R2(b) was written before it existed; the section-rename lifecycle that motivated (b) was not re-applied to the new field.
**Suggested fix:** Two words and one clause. In R2(b): "…an entry in `Where`, `Propagation sites`, **or `Discharged:`**…". In the § Design 7 block, after the verification sentence: "A `Discharged:` entry naming a section that no longer exists is re-anchored under R2(b), not a violation; file a P2 naming the entry if it carries no marker."
