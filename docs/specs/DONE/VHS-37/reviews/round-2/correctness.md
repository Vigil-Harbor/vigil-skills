### F-1: Design 1 step 0 enumerates three remedies for a `routing violation:` finding and omits the R2(c) discharge — the remedy the round-1 P0 fix created
**Severity:** P2
**Pre-ship recommended:** yes
**Where:** spec.md:86 (§ Design 1, step 0), against spec.md:126 (R2(c)) and spec.md:181 (§ Design 7 block)
**Claim:** Step 0: "A finding whose title begins `routing violation:` is never deferred: it is folded by **the R2 repair, R6 merge, or preamble insert it names**, whatever its site count or scope; disposition it `fixed: repaired D-<n>` (or `fixed: merged` / `fixed: preamble`)."
**Why this is wrong:** Design 7 files four distinct violation classes: `routing violation: missing preamble`, `routing violation: duplicate section`, a field-class `routing violation: D-<n>` ("naming the failing field or rule"), and the **ceiling class** — "A row whose `Finding` severity is `P0` and whose `Scope` is `in-scope`, and that carries no `Discharged:` field… file it once, as a P0 titled `routing violation: D-<n>`, **naming the ceiling**" (spec.md:181). Step 0's list maps cleanly onto the first three, but the ceiling class names no field, so "the R2 repair … it names" resolves to nothing; R2(a) is explicitly "repairing the **field** a `routing violation: D-<n>` finding names"; and R2's third edit is worded as "**discharging**", not repairing. The disposition vocabulary offered (`fixed: repaired D-<n>` / `fixed: merged` / `fixed: preamble`) likewise has no entry for it. Step 0 is the instruction the 2e author actually executes on this finding, and it is the one place the discharge is not reachable — which is exactly the class round-1 edge-cases/F-1 and correctness/F-1 were filed about. The mechanism is not lost (R2(c) is ~30 lines below in the same 2e insertion and is keyed to this finding by name), so this is confusion rather than deadlock, but it is confusion at the one step the round-1 P0 fix depends on. Two round-1 fixes — F-1's discharge and F-10's routing-violation carve-out — were folded independently and their intersection was never walked.
**Suggested fix:** In step 0, replace the remedy list with one that covers all four classes: "…it is folded by the R2 edit the violation calls for — the field repair it names (R2(a)), the R6 merge, the preamble insert, or, for a ceiling-class violation, the R2(c) discharge — whatever its site count or scope; disposition it `fixed: repaired D-<n>`, `fixed: merged`, `fixed: preamble`, or `fixed: discharged D-<n> — § <a>; § <b>`." Add the fourth phrase to § Design 4's disposition-phrase prose alongside `deferred:`, and extend test row 9 with `grep -cF 'fixed: discharged' skills/spec-cycle/SKILL.md` ≥ 1.

### F-3: The `Suggested fix` well-formedness test forbids the blank line CommonMark needs to close the blockquote — the markdown-correct row fails as a P0
**Severity:** P2
**Pre-ship recommended:** yes
**Where:** spec.md:115-117 (row shape) and spec.md:181 (§ Design 7 well-formedness test)
**Claim:** Design 7: "**every line between its `**Suggested fix:**` label and its `**Propagation sites:**` label begins with `>`**". The shape at :115-117 shows the `> `-quoted fix immediately followed, with no blank line, by `**Propagation sites:** …`.
**Why this is wrong:** In CommonMark a non-blank paragraph line directly after a blockquote line is a *lazy continuation* — `**Propagation sites:**`, and everything after it up to the next blank line, is absorbed **into** the blockquote. The shape as drawn is therefore markdown-incorrect, and the natural repair (a blank line before `**Propagation sites:**`) is exactly what the well-formedness test rejects: the blank line does not begin with `>`, so the row is not well-formed, so every lens files a P0 `routing violation: D-<n>`, and R2(a)'s only permitted repair is to remove the blank line and reinstate the lazy continuation. This is the same shape as the round-1 P0 (a rule whose only permitted remedy re-creates the violation), reached from the other side. It is also a near-certain trigger: the field is written by an LLM author formatting a multi-paragraph blockquote, and round-1 edge-cases/F-9 fixed only blank lines *inside* the fix text, not the boundary. Nothing else in the spec depends on the absence of that blank line — the section grammar keys on `> `-prefixed lines and level-2 headings (:104), neither of which a blank line affects.
**Suggested fix:** Make the test blank-tolerant at the boundary only, in both places it is stated. Design 7: "every **non-blank** line between its `**Suggested fix:**` label and its `**Propagation sites:**` label begins with `>`, and at most one blank line — the blockquote terminator — appears immediately before the `**Propagation sites:**` label; a blank line anywhere else between them is not well-formed." Mirror the allowance in the row shape at :115 ("a blank line is stored as a bare `>`; a single blank line closing the blockquote before `**Propagation sites:**` is permitted") and show it in the shape block. Extend test row 6 with `grep -cF 'non-blank line between' agents/spec-reviewer-*.md` → 1 per file.

### F-7: R2(c)'s trigger does not cover the branch where every lens files the underlying in-scope P0 instead of the ceiling violation
**Severity:** P3
**Where:** spec.md:126 (R2(c)), against spec.md:181 (§ Design 7 block) and spec.md:129 (R5)
**Claim:** R2(c) fires for "a row the reviewers filed a **ceiling-class `routing violation: D-<n>`** against, **or** whose `Scope` repair under (a) turns it into an in-scope P0 row."
**Why this is wrong:** Design 7 makes the ceiling violation conditional per lens — "file it once… **unless you are filing the underlying P0 under the exception below**, which cites the row and names the ceiling instead". If, in a given round, every dispatched lens takes that branch (all of them regenerate the same in-scope P0 from the spec text — the normal case for a defect all four can see), no ceiling-class violation is filed and no `Scope` repair occurs, so neither R2(c) trigger matches. The author folds the underlying P0 at every site per step 3, R5 says "the row stays", and the row still reads P0 / in-scope with no `Discharged:` field. Next round the underlying defect is fixed so no lens takes the exception branch, some lens files the ceiling violation, and the discharge finally runs — one round of the four-round budget spent on bookkeeping, in the skill whose purpose is to stop burning rounds on bookkeeping. Not a deadlock, and with three or four lenses at least one will usually file the ceiling violation in the same round, which is why this is P3 rather than higher.
**Suggested fix:** Broaden R2(c)'s first trigger to be state-based rather than finding-based: "(c) **discharging** a row whose `Finding` severity is `P0` and whose `Scope` is (or becomes, under an (a) repair) `in-scope` — whether the reviewers surfaced it as a ceiling-class `routing violation: D-<n>` or as the underlying in-scope P0 citing the row: fold the finding at every site on its `Propagation sites` list in the same round and append `**Discharged:** folded round <n> — § <a>; § <b>`." Add one clause to R5 so the fold-then-leave-the-row path points at (c): "a fold that resolves an in-scope-P0 row's root is completed by the R2(c) discharge in the same round."
